Solar & Subsidies

What is PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana and how much subsidy can I get? – +

PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana is the central government's flagship rooftop solar scheme launched in February 2024, targeting 1 crore households by March 2027. As an eligible residential consumer, you get a Central Financial Assistance (CFA) of up to ₹60,000* for 2 kW, and up to ₹78,000* for 3 kW and above (plus up to ₹30,000* Uttar Pradesh state subsidy, totaling up to ₹1,08,000*) — credited directly to your bank account via DBT after installation. You also become eligible for up to 300 free units of electricity per month.

Am I eligible for the PM Surya Ghar subsidy? – +

You are eligible if you are an Indian residential property owner with: (1) an active DISCOM electricity connection in your name, (2) a shadow-free rooftop of at least 100 sq. ft. per kW of capacity, and (3) no prior solar subsidy claimed at the same address. Government, commercial and industrial consumers are not eligible for this specific residential scheme but have other incentives available (Accelerated Depreciation, AD).

What additional state subsidies are available in Uttar Pradesh? – +

Up to ₹30,000* additional state subsidy for residential rooftop solar under the UP Solar Energy Policy. Net-metering is available through UPPCL DISCOMs (PVVNL, DVVNL, MVVNL, PuVVNL, KESCO) with seamless grid synchronization.

We layer the central PM Surya Ghar CFA with applicable state subsidies — and do all the paperwork for you.

How long is the payback period for rooftop solar? – +

For a typical 3 kW residential on-grid system in Uttar Pradesh after central subsidy: payback is usually 3.5 to 5 years. The system continues generating for another 20+ years, giving you essentially free electricity for two decades. Commercial & industrial systems often pay back in 3 to 4 years after factoring in Accelerated Depreciation tax benefits.

What is net-metering and how does it work? – +

Net-metering is a billing mechanism where a bi-directional meter measures both the electricity you consume from the grid and the surplus solar power you export back. At the end of each billing cycle, the DISCOM nets the two and you only pay for (or get credited for) the difference. We handle the entire net-metering application with your DISCOM (UPPCL & UP DISCOMs) — including the bi-directional meter installation. The maximum net-metering capacity is 1 MWp per consumer.

On-grid vs. off-grid vs. hybrid — which one is right for me? – +

On-grid (grid-tied): cheapest, exports surplus to the grid via net-metering, eligible for PM Surya Ghar subsidy — but stops working during a power cut. Best for cities with reliable supply.

Off-grid (battery-only): independent of the grid, runs entirely on solar + a battery bank. Higher cost. Best for villages, farms or remote sites without a reliable connection.

Hybrid (solar + battery + grid): rides through power cuts seamlessly, can still export surplus where rules allow. Higher upfront cost but best of both worlds — recommended for areas with frequent outages.

How much roof area do I need? – +

Roughly 100 sq. ft. of shadow-free roof per 1 kW of capacity. So a 3 kW system needs ~300 sq. ft., and a 10 kW system about 1,000 sq. ft. The roof should be free of major shadows between 9 AM and 4 PM. We assess this during the free site survey using a shadow-analysis tool.

How long do solar panels last? What is the warranty? – +

The high-efficiency solar modules we install carry a 25-year linear performance warranty (guaranteed to produce at least 80–85% of their rated output even at year 25) and a 10 to 12-year product warranty. String inverters typically come with a 5-year warranty (extendable to 10–25 years). Mounting structures are galvanised for 25+ years of corrosion resistance.

Will solar work during the monsoon or on cloudy days? – +

Yes — panels still generate, just at reduced output. A heavy monsoon day might produce 20–40% of a clear sunny day. Annual generation estimates already account for cloudy days, monsoon, and dust. Uttar Pradesh averages around 4.5–5.5 kWh per kW per day annualised — the math works in your favour.

What is a Solar Atta Chakki and how does it reduce operational costs? – +

A Solar Atta Chakki (Flour Mill) is powered directly by rooftop solar panels paired with a high-efficiency VFD solar inverter drive. It runs smoothly on clean solar power without requiring expensive commercial electricity or diesel generators, saving up to 80–90% on monthly milling costs with an attractive ROI payback period of 2–3 years.

Can I finance solar through a bank loan or EMI? – +

Yes. Most public-sector and several private banks offer rooftop solar loans at concessional rates under the PM Surya Ghar scheme — typically around 6.75% p.a. for systems up to 3 kW. We help you with documentation and connect you with our financing banking partners for low-EMI installation.

How long does the entire installation take? – +

Site survey to commissioning typically takes 3 – 6 weeks. The on-site installation itself is usually 2–4 days for a residential system. Most of the timeline is DISCOM net-metering paperwork and the bi-directional meter changeover, which we expedite as much as the utility allows.

Do you provide AMC after installation? – +

Yes. Every system we install includes 1 year of free AMC, with optional annual extensions starting from ₹500 / kW / year. AMC covers scheduled maintenance, inverter health checks, performance reports and remote monitoring through an app.
